\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Every backtest of a market-making strategy must decide whether a passive limit
order that never existed would have been filled. Historical data cannot answer
this, so backtests substitute a rule---most commonly, that an order fills once
the price touches it---and the substitution is rarely measured. We measure it.
Using the public order-level (L4) dataset of the Hyperliquid on-chain limit order
book, in which every order of every participant is observed from placement to
fill or cancellation, we reconstruct the book, the exact queue position of each
resting order and the moment it reaches the front of its queue, and we confront
four standard fill models with the real outcome of millions of real orders.
The touch rule overstates fills five- to six-fold; the volume-through rule
understates them two- to three-fold and misses more than half of real fills;
an exponential-intensity clock in the Avellaneda--Stoikov tradition is governed
by its calibration rather than by the data. All three errors share one cause:
more than 94\% of the volume queued ahead of an order leaves by cancellation, not
by trading. A queue-aware rule reproduces the observed fill rate to within 5\%,
and its residual error is entirely due to the exchange processing cancellations
before aggressors within a block; with that rule added it matches the observed
fill rate exactly. Such in-block cancellations rescue 2--4\% of orders that a
trade would otherwise have filled, and the rescued fills would have been worse
than real ones at short horizons. Backtesting four quoting strategies under each
fill rule, the same strategy's PnL changes by up to \tbd{} and the ranking of
strategies agrees across fill rules with Kendall $\tau$ = \tbd.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} market making; backtesting; fill probability; queue
position; limit order book; order-level data; decentralised exchanges.
\end{abstract}
