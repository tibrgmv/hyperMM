\section{Reconstruction details}
\label{app:reconstruction}

\paragraph{Representation.}
Prices and sizes are decoded into 64-bit integers in units of $10^{-8}$ rather
than into floating point. The queue-aware rule fires when the volume ahead
reaches \emph{exactly} zero, and floating-point accumulation leaves residues of
order $10^{-16}$ that make the condition fail. Decimal strings are parsed by digit
shifting, exact by construction. Products of two scaled quantities (notional,
VWAP) are formed only after division by the scale, since they would overflow.
Times are integer nanoseconds on one scale across streams.

\paragraph{Blocks.}
In the pilot hour \BTC{} records fall on 37\,301 distinct timestamps and \SOL{}
records on 36\,366 (mean spacing 96.5 and 99.0\ms); the two sets share 35\,818.
If timestamps were event times, coincidences within the $3.6\times10^{12}$
nanoseconds of an hour would be expected $4\times10^{-4}$ times. The diagnostic
was first validated on synthetic block- and event-clocked data.

\paragraph{Processing order within a block.}
Measured over all pairs of events of two types within one block---a statistic,
unlike the share of ``clean'' blocks, that does not depend on block
size---placements precede cancellations in 96.6\% (\BTC, 155 million pairs) and
98.5\% (\SOL, 19.9 million) of pairs, and cancellations precede fills in 98.8\%
and 99.4\%. The documented order (cancellations before new orders) is thus not
the observed one, while its economically relevant part holds. Line order is not
explained by sorting on order id.

\paragraph{Time.}
A placement takes the time the order was accepted, a removal the time of the
order's last status, and a size reduction inherits the last exact time before it.
Pairing the $k$-th book change with the $k$-th status instead would create false
pairs, since the streams record different numbers of events for 0.87\% of orders.
99.45\% of \SOL{} changes receive an exact time; inherited times are within one
block of the truth in 92.6\% of verifiable cases.

\paragraph{Front of the queue.}
Orders of a level are numbered by arrival and a head pointer moves past orders
that have left, never past orders still to come; the pass is linear and compiled
with \texttt{numba}. On 200 random orders per instrument it agrees with an
independent computation of the full path of volume ahead in 200 of 200.

\paragraph{The status stream omits maker fills.}
For maker orders with at least one trade, \texttt{filled} statuses cover 58.2\%
(\SOL) and 61.6\% (\BTC) of trades and never exceed them. A status is recorded for
the taker in 96.0\% / 94.6\% of trades but for the maker in only 63.8\% / 68.9\%.

\paragraph{Reduce-only orders.}
The exchange trims a reduce-only order to the current position: the size in the
book differs from the submitted one for 81\% of such \SOL{} orders and almost
never otherwise. Queue volume is therefore taken from the book stream, and a size
reduction without a trade is a trim, not a fill.

\paragraph{Hour boundaries.}
The book-diff stream carries changes, not snapshots, so an hourly file read alone
opens on an empty book and its first minutes are a warm-up. Hours are rebuilt in
a window chaining the previous and next files; the files join without a single
contradiction at the seam, and cold-start events in the centre hour fall by
three-quarters.
