\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Venue}
Hyperliquid runs a central limit order book for perpetual futures on its own
blockchain. Orders, cancellations and trades are processed in blocks produced
roughly every 100\ms; within a block the exchange applies a fixed processing
order. Price--time priority governs each level.

\subsection{The Open Book dataset}
\citet{albers2026openbook} publish three hourly streams for \BTC, \ETH{} and \SOL:
\begin{description}
  \item[Order statuses.] Every status change of every order (open, cancelled,
  filled, rejected for one of several reasons), in a packed 54-byte binary
  record carrying a nanosecond timestamp \texttt{ts}, the order id
  \texttt{oid}, side, limit price, size, time in force and reduce-only flag.
  Rejected orders are stored in separate files.
  \item[Book diffs.] Every change to the resting book---an order added, reduced
  or removed---as JSON, \emph{without any timestamp}: the position of a line in
  the file is the only ordering it carries.
  \item[Trades.] Every trade with both counterparties' order ids, price, size
  and time, for all listed instruments.
\end{description}
The order id is the only key common to all three streams. Coverage is December
2025 for order statuses and book diffs, and October 2025 to January 2026 for
trades.

\subsection{Sample}
The results in this draft use one hour, \pilothour, for \BTC{} and \SOL{}:
5.2 million status records for \BTC{} and 1.2 million for \SOL, 6.6 million book
changes across the three instruments, and 210\,567 trades across all listed
instruments. The full sample covers \tbd{} hours of December 2025 for \BTC,
\ETH{} and \SOL{}: \tbd{} million passive orders and \tbd{} million trades.

\paragraph{Composition.}
The order flow is overwhelmingly that of liquidity providers: 97.2\% of
accepted \SOL{} orders are post-only (ALO), 1.4\% immediate-or-cancel and
1.2\% good-till-cancelled. Only 0.83\% of passive \SOL{} orders and 1.60\% of
passive \BTC{} orders receive any fill (Section~\ref{sec:outcomes}). The spread
equals one tick 93.5\% (\SOL) and 90.3\% (\BTC) of the time, so competition for
fills is waged through queue position rather than price.
