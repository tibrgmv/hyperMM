\subsection{What is knowable at placement}
\label{sec:knowable}
\label{sec:survival}
\label{sec:ceiling}
The rules so far read the market \emph{after} the order was placed. A strategy
deciding where to quote needs the answer before: how soon is an order placed now
likely to be filled? Most orders are withdrawn before they could have been
filled (Section~\ref{sec:outcomes}), so this is a time-to-event problem under
heavy censoring. We fit two models on features known just before placement---side,
size, volume ahead, distance from the best price and from the mid, spread,
imbalance, and recent trading and price moves, on a logarithmic scale
(Appendix~\ref{app:survival})---and compare them with the exponential clock, which
uses the distance from the mid alone. The first is a Cox proportional-hazards
model. The second, gradient-boosted trees for the discrete-time fill hazard, is
the strongest model we could fit, and its held-out score is our estimate of the
\emph{ceiling}: what this description of the book supports. Models are fitted on
hours 0--10 and scored on hours 12--23, with hour 11 left out as a buffer.

An order cancelled before it was filled is not a miss; its fill time is only
known to exceed its life. We use the time-dependent concordance $C$ of
\citet{antolini2005time}, the censoring-weighted AUC at three horizons, and the
right-censored log-likelihood, a proper scoring rule \citep{arroyo2024deep}, as a
skill against the marginal Kaplan--Meier curve. The weighting assumes that
cancellation is independent of the fill time, which the previous subsection
shows to be false; the bias is common to the models compared.

\paragraph{The ceiling is below one.}
On held-out hours the boosted model reaches a concordance of 0.92--0.93 and an
AUC of 0.92--0.97 (Table~\ref{tab:exante}); four further features move it by at
most 1.2\% of the attainable. The Cox model recovers 93--97\% of the attainable
concordance. The exponential clock recovers 71\%: its ranking is serviceable,
but its probabilities are not---its log-likelihood skill is negative on every
instrument ($-1.0$ to $-2.2$), worse than ignoring the order's features.

\paragraph{Most of an order's fate is settled after it is placed.}
Scored against the realised outcome as the rules of Table~\ref{tab:expost} are,
boosted trees on placement-time features rank orders well (AUC 0.87--0.93) but
their Brier skill is 0.01--0.07 on \ETH{} and \SOL{} and 0.24 on \BTC; the
queue-aware rule, with the market record in hand, reaches 0.996. At placement one
can tell which orders are more likely to be filled, hardly which ones will be.
