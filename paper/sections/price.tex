\section{A second substitution: the price of aggressive orders}
\label{sec:price}

A candle-based backtest substitutes an invented number at two points, and they
differ by order type. For a limit order it invents the \emph{decision}---whether
the bar touched the price---while the price, the order's own limit, is right;
this is \touch{} expressed in bars, measured in Section~\ref{sec:fillmodels}.
For a market order the decision is trivial, but the \emph{price} is invented:
common platforms execute it at the next bar's open, ignore volume, and add a
user-chosen constant slippage. The spread is never crossed and the book is never
walked. A market maker is passive, but any inventory-constrained strategy unloads
aggressively, and in a candle backtest that unloading is free.

We estimate the true price of a market order by sweeping the reconstructed book
at the moment of decision, consuming levels outward from the best price until the
size is filled, and compare it with candle-based approximations (mid-price, best
price, bar VWAP, bar close, next bar's open), signed so that a positive error is
a gift to the strategy.

Two identities hold exactly on the data (residual below $10^{-15}$):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The gift of a round trip equals the mean spread plus the cost of two
  sweeps through the book. Price drift between decision and execution helps one
  leg exactly as much as it hurts the other and cancels; what remains is
  structural.
  \item The difference between the sell and the buy legs equals twice the drift
  plus the difference in sweep cost between the two sides of the book.
\end{enumerate}
The bias is positive in every combination of instrument, size and side. The
fabricated saving per round trip is 1.33--3.01 median spreads on \SOL{} and
3.12--6.37 on \BTC, increasing with order size; it exceeds the market maker's
entire gross revenue from the same round trip already at \$1\,000. By the first
identity it is bounded below by the full spread at any size and does not average
out with more data. Over the full sample the saving is \tbd--\tbd{} median
spreads on \BTC, \tbd--\tbd{} on \ETH{} and \tbd--\tbd{} on \SOL.
